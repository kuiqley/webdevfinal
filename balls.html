The Evolution of the Golf Ball

Golf balls have come a long way since the 14th century when the sport was first introduced, and today, they have become so modernized and well-made, that they have have even had to change the way they are made because they were going so far in professional torunaments.From rocks to wood and other creations, the origins of the golf ball can be traced back to five distinct stages.

Historic Milestones

  • Wooden Balls (1300s): The first iteration was the wooden golf ball.Carved from hardwood, mostly beech. Extremely inconsistent in flight. However, it is up for debate if they were really used in golf, or just other stick and ball games.
  • Hairy Golf Balls (1400's - 1800's): The second iteration was the Hairy golf ball. THis was a ball imported from the Netherlands that was hand-sewn around a leather ball filled with cows hair or straw. They were the most common ball for years to come, as they were the cheapest, and used up until the 18th century.
  • Faetherie Golf Balls (1600's - 1800's): The third iteration was the Faetherie golf ball. It was similar to the hairy golf ball, but this one was filled with goose or chicken feather, and was able to be stuffed more than the hairy version. However, in order to make these balls, workers had to feathers and leather had to be shaoped while wet, and then be worked with, then dried out, on repeat. It was a very time consuming process, making these very expensive to purchase.
  • Guttie/Gutta Golf Balls (1800's - 1900's): The Guttie ball was inbded in 1848 by Dr. Robert Adams Paterson. This ball was made by using dried sap from the Malaysian Sapodilla tree which had a rubber-like quality and if it was heated up, could be molded into a ball shape. This ball quickly gained popularity becuase it was less expensive to make, and had better flight properties than the Faetherie ball.
  • Rubber Core Golf Balls (1900's - Present): It was in 1898 that Coburn Haskell discovered that if he created a ball out of rubber thread, it would bounce. This led to the birth of the rubber Haskell golf ball. Which had a solid round core wound with rubber, then covered in a shell of balata sap. Then in the mid 1960's we discovered as new resin called Surlyn, that we started using to cover golf balls as it didn't leave indents. Today, the rubber Haskel ball is the typical standard for golf balls.

Modern Golf Ball Types

Distance Balls

Designed for maximum carry and roll. Great for beginners or high handicappers who want more yardage. Usually harder with less spin.

Spin Balls

Engineered for control and short game finesse. Offers higher spin around the greens for more stopping power, favored by skilled players.

Soft Feel Balls

Provide a more comfortable and responsive feel, especially on putts and chips. Typically have lower compression for slower swing speeds.

<h1>Getting Started with Golf</h1>
<p>Golf can seem intimidating at first, but with the right equipment and a few key tips, you'll be swinging like a pro in no time!(not really though lol, golf is incredibly difficult, and it will take you a long time)</p>

<div class="accordion" id="golfGuideAccordion">

	<div class="accordion-item">
    		<h2 class="accordion-header" id="headingOne">
      			<button class="accordion-button" type="button" data-bs-toggle="collapse" data-bs-target="#collapseOne">
        			1. Choose the Right Clubs
      			</button>
    		</h2>
    		<div id="collapseOne" class="accordion-collapse collapse show" data-bs-parent="#golfGuideAccordion">
      			<div class="accordion-body">
        			When you are first beginning, getting the right clubs might seem intimidating. However, it is a lot easier, and cheaper than you think. When you get your first set of clubs, I recommend going to look for a set that has a driver, a few irons, a pitching wedge, and a putter. You can find these in a variety of places, including Facebook marketplace, secondhand sporting goods stores, or friends and family. Make sure that when you are getting your first set, you don't spend too much money on them, because it is not worth it. 
      			</div>
    		</div>
  	</div>

	<div class="accordion-item">
    		<h2 class="accordion-header" id="headingTwo">
      			<button class="accordion-button collapsed" type="button" data-bs-toggle="collapse" data-bs-target="#collapseTwo">
        			2. Watch Videos
      			</button>
    		</h2>
    		<div id="collapseTwo" class="accordion-collapse collapse" data-bs-parent="#golfGuideAccordion">
      			<div class="accordion-body">
        			Before you start swinging, I recommend watching a few YouTube videos on how to hold and swing a golf club. In my opinion, I find it best to start with figuring out your grip and your stance, before figuring out your swing, as they are fundamental and helping you make contact with the ball. There are thousands of YouTube and TikTok videos out there you can use for advice on this. I would avoid taking lessons, as they can be very expensive and as a beginner, may overwhelm you. 
      			</div>
    		</div>
  	</div>

	<div class="accordion-item">
    		<h2 class="accordion-header" id="headingThree">
      			<button class="accordion-button collapsed" type="button" data-bs-toggle="collapse" data-bs-target="#collapseThree">
        			3. Learn Basic Golf Etiquette
      			</button>
    		</h2>
    		<div id="collapseThree" class="accordion-collapse collapse" data-bs-parent="#golfGuideAccordion">
      			<div class="accordion-body">
        			Respect is the most important rule of golf. You want to respect yourself, your equipment, and the game. Learning golf etiquette isn't difficult, but some tips that I have would be: fix your divots, respect the course, and keep pace of play. You don't want to be taking more than about 15 minutes a hole, and if someone behind you is obviously waiting for you every hole, be kind and let them play ahead of you. 
      			</div>
    		</div>
  	</div>

	<div class="accordion-item">
    		<h2 class="accordion-header" id="headingFour">
      			<button class="accordion-button collapsed" type="button" data-bs-toggle="collapse" data-bs-target="#collapseFour">
        			4. Practice Short Game
      			</button>
    		</h2>
    		<div id="collapseFour" class="accordion-collapse collapse" data-bs-parent="#golfGuideAccordion">
      			<div class="accordion-body">
        			While it is important to have a good long game, it is even more critical to have a good short game. A majority of your shots are going to be with your wedges, your chippers, and your putter. There are plenty of courses and ranges around that have putting greens and chipping areas, as well as a few short wedge shot holes. Take some time to visit one of these and get a feel for the distances on your clubs, and maybe even watch some videos on how the pro's read a green and hit their putter, as there is a lot more that goes into it than it seems at first glance. 
      			</div>
    		</div>
  	</div>
